What researchers mean by plant bioacoustics

Researchers have reported ultrasonic airborne sounds from plants under particular experimental conditions. A 2023 study in Cell investigated sounds associated with stressed plants. A 2024 viewpoint in New Phytologist discusses why the evidence for plant acoustic communication remains a question for continued research.

Detecting an acoustic event is not the same as knowing its cause, biological meaning, or intended recipient. Plant studies require suitable microphones, controlled recording conditions, comparison groups, and independent validation. Many ultrasonic signals also exceed the useful capture range of ordinary phone microphones.

The current ZionCore lab is a general acoustic frequency analyzer. It does not include a calibrated ultrasonic receiver, a plant-stress classifier, or a species-identification system.

Build a careful environmental recording

  • Keep microphone position, distance, device, and gain settings consistent.
  • Record a room or garden “blank” before and after the target recording.
  • Repeat captures and note weather, watering, nearby appliances, and animal activity.
  • Use a microphone with a documented response range for frequencies beyond ordinary audible sound.
  • Describe a pattern before assigning its source; compare alternate explanations.
