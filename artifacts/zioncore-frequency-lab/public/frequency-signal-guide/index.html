Frequency is one way to describe how quickly a repeating waveform cycles. An audio spectrum analyzer helps reveal which frequency components appear in a captured sound and how they change.

Four ideas that make a spectrum easier to read

Frequency

Measured in hertz (Hz), frequency counts cycles per second. A single tone may produce a strong peak, while everyday sounds often contain many components.

FFT resolution

The FFT divides a sampled recording window into frequency bins. Resolution is approximately sample rate divided by FFT size; a larger FFT separates nearby peaks more finely but uses a longer time window.

Harmonics

Harmonic peaks occur near whole-number multiples of a fundamental frequency. Their spacing can help describe a pattern, but does not by itself identify the source.

Spectrogram

A spectrogram places frequency against time and uses intensity to show relative spectral strength. It can reveal changing tones, repeated events, or broad-band activity.

From measurement to interpretation

A sound frequency analyzer can describe peaks, relative levels, harmonics, and temporal patterns. These measurements are different from decoded message content, a source hypothesis, or a spiritual interpretation. Keeping those layers separate makes room for both careful analysis and personal meaning.
