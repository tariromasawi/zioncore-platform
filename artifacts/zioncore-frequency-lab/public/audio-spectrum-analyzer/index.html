How FFT frequency analysis works

The Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) converts a short sampled waveform into frequency bins. ZionCore displays the strongest measured peaks and related features using the selected FFT size. Frequency resolution depends on the sample rate and FFT size: sample rate divided by the number of FFT points.

A longer FFT can separate nearby frequencies more finely, while a shorter window responds more quickly to changes. The analyzer offers 2,048, 4,096, and 8,192-point settings so you can compare these trade-offs on your own recording.

The microphone records acoustic sound. It can measure a tone played by a speaker, but it is not a direct radio-frequency receiver. A frequency value describes the captured pattern; it does not identify who or what created it.

Questions about sound analysis

Does ZionCore upload my recording?

No. Live capture and local file analysis run in the browser. A requested ZionCore interpretation sends derived measurements and your chosen context, not raw audio.

Can an audio spectrum analyzer identify the source?

It can describe measured frequency patterns. Source identification needs additional evidence and cannot be established from a peak alone.

Can a phone microphone analyze radio signals?

No. A phone microphone measures acoustic pressure within its supported audio range. Direct RF measurement requires compatible receiver hardware.
